Protocol Specification & Architecture

LinkProof provides cryptographic authenticity for physical QR codes and short URLs, mitigating phishing, tampering, and redirection attacks.

1. What LinkProof Does

Physical QR codes (stickers on parking meters, campus portals, restaurant menus) and shortened links can easily be replaced, modified, or redirected to malicious destinations. While HTTPS encrypts browser traffic, HTTPS alone does not prove that a website was the destination intended by the publisher.

LinkProof solves this by binding the destination URL, issuer identity, timestamps, and approved redirect policies into a canonical record digitally signed with ECDSA NIST P-256. Verifiers authenticate the publisher, check record validity, and audit every redirect hop before allowing connections.

2. How Signed QR Verification Works

Step 1: Canonical Serialization

Records are normalized using RFC 8785 JSON canonicalization (alphabetical keys, no whitespace) to ensure signature determinism.

Step 2: ECDSA Signature

The publisher signs SHA-256(canonical_bytes) with their SECP256R1 private key. The resulting ASN.1 DER signature is Base64 encoded.

Step 3: Trust Decoupling

Mathematical validity does not imply trust. The verifier checks if the signer's public key fingerprint is explicitly trusted in the local trust store.

3. What Redirect & TLS Auditing Checks

  • Independent Hop Execution: Automatic redirects are disabled. Each HTTP 3xx hop is inspected individually for status code, headers, and relative paths.
  • Pre-Flight SSRF Protection: Hostnames are resolved before connection and checked against private (RFC 1918), loopback (127.0.0.0/8, ::1), and cloud link-local (169.254.169.254) addresses.
  • Loop & Hop Boundaries: Cyclic redirects are aborted immediately. Maximum hops are restricted to prevent denial-of-service.
  • TLS Certificate Chain: Validates Subject Alternative Names (SANs), certificate expiry, issuer CA trust, and hostname matching at every HTTPS hop.

4. Known Limitations & Security Assumptions

HTTPS is not Phishing Reputation:

A successful TLS handshake and valid certificate only proves encrypted transit to the server named in the certificate. It does not prove that the website is benevolent, harmless, or free from phishing.

Google Safe Browsing Integration:

LinkProof supports real-time phishing and malware lookups via the Google Safe Browsing API v4 using the GOOGLE_SAFE_BROWSING_API_KEY environment variable. The engine returns structured states: THREAT_DETECTED, NO_KNOWN_THREAT, NOT_CHECKED, or CHECK_UNAVAILABLE. If unconfigured, it defaults safely to NOT_CHECKED.

Trust Store Synchronization:

Offline verification validates cryptographic signatures against previously imported keys. However, offline mode cannot detect real-time key revocations or website compromise until the trust store is updated.
